> **Executive Briefing Memorandum**  
> The Delaware Chancery Court ordered specific performance compelling Nano Dimension to close its $580 million acquisition of Desktop Metal. Chancellor McCormick ruled that deliberate CFIUS clearance delays orchestrated by an activist board bloc breached contractual reasonable best efforts under DGCL Section 251, instantly compressing deal arbitrage spreads by 340 basis points.

---

### Procedural Ruling: Chancery Docket C.A. No. 2024-0821-KSJM

In a landmark 74-page post-trial memorandum opinion, Chancellor Kathaleen St. J. Jude McCormick of the Delaware Court of Chancery ordered the extraordinary equitable remedy of **specific performance**, compelling hesitant acquirer Nano Dimension Ltd. (`NASDAQ: NNDM`) to consummate its **$580 million** merger agreement with additive manufacturing target Desktop Metal Inc. (`NYSE: DM`).

The court rejected Nano Dimension’s defense that closing conditions were rendered impossible by pending national security review before the Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States (CFIUS). Instead, Chancellor McCormick determined that after an activist shareholder slate seized control of Nano’s board, the acquirer deliberately engaged in regulatory slow-walking to force Desktop Metal into corporate insolvency.

### Contractual "Reasonable Best Efforts" Doctrine

The decision establishes vital transaction guidance for corporate M&A practitioners navigating closing conditions and regulatory approvals under Delaware law:
- **Contractual Sabotage**: When an acquirer covenants to use "reasonable best efforts" to obtain antitrust or foreign investment clearances, it cannot manufacture regulatory friction to engineer a walk-away right.
- **Evidentiary Discovery**: Internal communications between activist directors and regulatory counsel revealed directives to withhold requested filings from CFIUS staff, constituting bad faith under Delaware General Corporation Law (DGCL) § 251.
- **Specific Performance Enforcement**: Delaware courts maintain a strong public policy presumption that monetary damages fail to adequately remedy target stockholders facing distressed liquidation upon an unjustified merger termination.

### Arbitrage Spreads & Deal Mechanics

Following release of the Chancellor's bench ruling:
- **Spread Compression**: Gross arbitrage spread collapsed from $1.85 to $0.42 per share (narrowing by 340 basis points).
- **Target Valuation Rally**: Shares of Desktop Metal surged 28.6% in high-volume institutional trading.
- **Reverse Break Fee Extinguishment**: Nano Dimension’s claim to a $25 million target termination fee was dismissed with prejudice, with the court reserving jurisdiction to assess attorneys' fees under the bad-faith exception.
