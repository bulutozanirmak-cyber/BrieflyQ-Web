> **Executive Briefing Memorandum (BLUF)**  
> The DOJ Antitrust Division and Office of the Comptroller of the Currency issued civil investigative demands targeting Capital One’s $35.3 billion acquisition of Discover Financial. Regulators warned that combining dual-issuance networks violates Clayton Act Section 7, expanding market gross arbitrage spreads by 480 basis points amid imminent federal preliminary injunction proceedings.

---

### The Regulatory Friction: Washington Banking Taskforce

In a dual-agency enforcement initiative unsealed in Washington, the Department of Justice Antitrust Division under Assistant Attorney General Jonathan Kanter, alongside the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC), served formal Civil Investigative Demands (CIDs) on Capital One Financial Corp (`NYSE: COF`) regarding its proposed **$35.3 billion** all-stock acquisition of Discover Financial Services (`NYSE: DFS`).

The regulatory review represents the most consequential test of the 2023 Merger Guidelines in the financial services sector, scrutinizing both credit card loan balance concentration and the vertical integration of Discover’s proprietary Pulse debit and credit payment routing rails.

### Clayton Act Section 7 & Subprime Concentration Theories

Antitrust authorities have outlined two primary structural competitive liabilities:
- **Subprime Credit Tier Concentration**: The combined bank would control over 31% of outstanding revolving credit card debt within the non-prime retail segment, creating an alleged pricing monopsony over interchange fees.
- **Network Exclusivity Rules**: Regulators are investigating whether Capital One's plan to migrate its 100M+ consumer accounts from Visa and Mastercard onto the Discover proprietary network constitutes an unlawful tying arrangement under Section 1 of the Sherman Act.
- **The Bank Merger Act Review**: Under 12 U.S.C. § 1828(c), the OCC must assess systemic financial stability risks, with public interest groups demanding a formal administrative hearing.

### Arbitrage Desk Pricing & Reverse Break Fee

Wall Street risk-arbitrage desks are pricing significant closing delays:
- **Spread Expansion**: Discover's gross merger spread widened from $9.20 to $17.60 (expanding 480 bps to an annualized discount of 14.8%).
- **Implied Probability**: Arbitrage models price a 56% probability of closure without extensive divestitures.
- **Reverse Termination Fee**: The definitive merger agreement contains a **$1.38 billion** reverse termination fee payable by Capital One if regulatory approvals fail prior to the statutory drop-dead date.
