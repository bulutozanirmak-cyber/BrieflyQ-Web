> **Executive Briefing Memorandum**  
> Vice Chancellor J. Travis Laster enjoined incumbent directors of Kestrel Systems from enforcing recently amended advance notice bylaws against an institutional activist slate. The Court held that imposing subjective questionnaire requirements and ten-day compliance windows during an active contest violates the fundamental stockholder franchise under DGCL § 211 and Blasius review.

---

### Judicial Benchmark: Protecting Fundamental Stockholder Franchise

In a 68-page ruling upholding the primacy of the shareholder vote, Vice Chancellor J. Travis Laster struck down defensive advance notice bylaw amendments adopted by Kestrel Systems Group in response to a multi-billion-dollar activist requisition.

The Court determined that while boards possess statutory authority under DGCL § 109 to enact orderly meeting procedures, deploying newly tightened disclosure traps to disqualify competing dissident nominees without compelling justification constitutes an inequitable manipulation of corporate machinery under Schnell and Blasius.

### The Boundaries of Legitimate vs. Preclusive Bylaw Provisions

The Court systematically evaluated each disputed bylaw amendment, categorizing their real-world impact on corporate democracy:

- Preclusive Questionnaire Demands: Requiring activist nominees to submit over 120 pages of proprietary investor relations data within 10 calendar days was deemed deliberately preclusive.
- Expanding Acting in Concert Definitions: Overbroad definitions treating informal communications among institutional asset managers as undisclosed voting blocks were invalidated as unreasonable restraints on collective action.
- Enhanced Scrutiny Re-Affirmed: Delaware courts will not accord business judgment deference to bylaw amendments adopted on a cloudy day when board entrenchment motivations are apparent.

### Strategic Implications for Corporate Boards & Activism Funds

The decision establishes clear boundaries for proxy defense advisors:

- 'Clear Day' Bylaw Audits: Corporate boards must modernize bylaws well ahead of activist engagement rather than reacting during active solicitation windows.
- Director Liability Exposure: Directors persisting with bad-faith disqualification tactics risk personal liability and attorney fee shifting under the bad faith exception.
- Institutional Investor Voting Strength: Activist managers gain renewed leverage to enforce board accountability in upcoming annual meeting cycles.
