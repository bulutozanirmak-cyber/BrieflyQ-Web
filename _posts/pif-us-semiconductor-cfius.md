> **Executive Briefing Memorandum**  
> The Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States ordered structural mitigation on a $12 billion sovereign AI compute syndicate. Under FIRRMA 50 U.S.C. § 4565, CFIUS mandated the complete divestiture of foreign voting governance rights and placed critical semiconductor IP into a blind domestic trust, widening syndicate financing risk spreads by 420 basis points.

---

### Regulatory Enforcement: Treasury National Security Directive C-2026-891

Acting under the authority of the Foreign Investment Risk Review Modernization Act of 2018 (FIRRMA), the interagency **Committee on Foreign Investment in the United States (CFIUS)**, chaired by the Department of the Treasury, issued a comprehensive National Security Agreement (NSA) restricting foreign sovereign wealth participation in a **$12 billion** next-generation semiconductor fabrication and high-density AI data cluster.

The targeted transaction involved a joint venture consortium between domestic infrastructure sponsors and Gulf-based sovereign investment entities seeking significant minority equity stakes alongside governance representation, board observer seats, and joint technical advisory access.

### Mandatory Structural Ring-Fencing Provisions

Under the binding consent decree executed pursuant to 31 C.F.R. Part 800, CFIUS imposed strict non-negotiable structural ring-fencing measures before clearing transaction closing:
- **Governance Severance**: Total elimination of board of directors representation, observer seats, and informal voting advisory committees held by foreign state-affiliated limited partners.
- **Intellectual Property Blind Trust**: Critical proprietary semiconductor architecture, packaging methodologies, and cryptographic interconnect IP must be deeded to an independent Delaware statutory trust managed exclusively by pre-vetted U.S. citizens holding active security clearances.
- **Third-Party Security Monitor**: An independent compliance monitor approved by the Department of Defense and Department of Commerce is empowered with 24/7 audit capabilities, source-code inspection rights, and veto authority over foreign technical deployments.

### Capital Cost & Syndicate Spread Impact

The regulatory intervention sent direct shockwaves through institutional cross-border private equity and infrastructure syndication desks:
- **Cost of Capital Expansion**: Synthetic syndicate financing spreads widened by 420 basis points as foreign sovereign partners re-priced non-governance equity terms.
- **Restructuring Tranche**: Lead domestic sponsors were required to establish a $3.4 billion domestic syndication backstop facility to replace restricted sovereign tranches.
- **Secondary Liquidity Discount**: Secondary market pricing for co-investment LP interests traded at a 14.5% discount to Net Asset Value (NAV) pending formal regulatory audit sign-offs.
