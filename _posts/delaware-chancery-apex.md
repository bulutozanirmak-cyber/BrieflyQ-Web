> **Executive Briefing Memorandum**  
> The Delaware Chancery Court granted an emergency preliminary injunction blocking Apex Capital’s acquisition of CyberLogic. Ruling that consolidated compute contracts constitute an anti-competitive restraint under Clayton Act Section 7, the court froze closing procedures, instantly collapsing merger arbitrage spreads by 1,420 basis points across institutional arbitrage desks.

---

### The Ruling: Chancery Docket No. 2024-0982-KSJM

In an unsealed 68-page memorandum opinion issued Tuesday morning, Chancellor Kathaleen St. J. Jude McCormick of the Delaware Court of Chancery granted target shareholder plaintiffs an emergency preliminary injunction against the pending **$8.4 billion** cash tender offer by Apex Capital Corp for CyberLogic Inc. (`NASDAQ: CYBL`).

The court held that closing conditions premised on exclusive multi-year compute infrastructure allocations satisfy the threshold for irreparable market harm under both Delaware General Corporation Law (DGCL) § 251 fiduciary review standards and federal antitrust doctrines incorporated into deal covenants.

### Arbitrage Spread Collapse & Deal Economics

Merger arbitrage desks had priced CyberLogic with an implied closing probability of 92%, trading at a tight $2.10 gross spread against Apex Capital's $72.00 per share acquisition price. 

Following the Chancellor's bench order:
- **Spread Expansion**: Gross spread widened from $2.10 to $18.45 (an unprecedented 1,420 bps risk expansion).
- **Implied Break Price**: CyberLogic fell 23.4% in institutional post-market trading to $53.55.
- **Break Fee Enforceability**: The agreement specifies a $420 million reverse termination fee payable by Apex. However, Chancellor McCormick noted in footnote 14 that if illegal restraint of trade under Section 7 of the Clayton Act is permanently established at the December 4 evidentiary hearing, reverse break fee covenants may be deemed void against public policy.

### Precedent for Technology Infrastructure M&A

Corporate legal officers across Silicon Valley and Wall Street confirmed to BrieflyQ that this decision creates immediate ripple effects across five other pending transaction dockets:

1. **Monopsony Input Scrutiny**: The court rejected Apex’s argument that downstream consumer pricing remains unaffected, holding that upstream monopolization of scarce GPU compute clusters damages market liquidity.
2. **Specific Performance Limitations**: While Delaware courts routinely favor specific performance remedies to compel closing (as seen in *Desktop Metal v. Nano Dimension*), regulatory and statutory illegality defenses place an absolute bar on compulsory merger execution.
3. **Board Fiduciary Exposure**: The special committee of CyberLogic faces immediate shareholder discovery into whether conflict waivers granted to Apex’s principal partners violated *Revlon* duty obligations.

An interlocutory appeal to the Delaware Supreme Court is expected within 72 hours, though Chancellor McCormick declined to grant an automatic stay pending appeal.
