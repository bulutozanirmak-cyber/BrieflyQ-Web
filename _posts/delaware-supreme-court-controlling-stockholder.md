> **Executive Briefing Memorandum**  
> The Delaware Supreme Court ruled en banc that all transactions where a controlling stockholder receives a non-ratable benefit require entire fairness review. Under DGCL § 141 and § 251, Chief Justice Seitz affirmed that absent simultaneous MFW independent committee and minority stockholder approvals, judicial scrutiny cannot revert to the deferential business judgment rule.

---

### Appellate Precedent: Delaware Supreme Court Docket No. 368, 2023

In an eagerly anticipated *en banc* decision shaping the contours of cross-border corporate governance and multi-billion-dollar sponsor buyouts, the **Supreme Court of the State of Delaware** resolved a decade of conflicting Chancery Court jurisprudence regarding standard of review in controlling stockholder transactions.

Writing for a unanimous court, Chief Justice Collins J. Seitz Jr. ruled that whenever a controlling stockholder stands on both sides of a corporate merger or extracts a disparate benefit to the detriment of minority investors, Delaware law presumes the application of Delaware's most rigorous standard: **entire fairness**.

The court firmly rejected arguments advanced by private equity sponsors and special committee practitioners seeking to restrict entire fairness solely to freeze-out squeeze-out cash mergers. Under the Court’s definitive holding, any corporate restructuring, carve-out recapitalization, or asset purchase conferring unique governance or economic advantages to a controller falls squarely within the entire fairness doctrine.

### The Strict *MFW* Cleansing Framework

To regain the protections of the business judgment rule—and secure early pre-trial dismissals under Rule 12(b)(6)—boards and dealmakers must scrupulously comply *ab initio* with the dual procedural safeguards articulated in *Kahn v. M&F Worldwide Corp.* (*MFW*):
- **Independent Special Committee**: Unconditional delegation of full negotiating power and veto authority to a fully empowered, unconflicted committee of independent directors prior to substantive deal terms being exchanged.
- **Majority-of-the-Minority Vote**: An uncoerced, fully informed approval condition by a majority of unaffiliated public stockholders, non-waivable from transaction inception.
- **Timing and Initiation Threshold**: The court underscored that introducing an independent committee or minority vote later in transaction negotiations fails to cleanse controller self-dealing retroactively.

### M&A Structuring Implications & Board Governance

The Supreme Court’s definitive mandate immediately reverberates across Wall Street advisory desks and Wilmington corporate courtrooms:
- **Litigation Risk Premiums**: Dual-class technology mergers and founder-led takeovers lacking *MFW* structuring face near-certain discovery phases, dramatically increasing settlement pressure.
- **Transaction Timelines**: Financial sponsors must budget an additional 4 to 8 weeks for independent committee formation, financial advisor retention, and minority shareholder solicitation.
- **Special Committee Independence**: Scrutiny over social, familial, and cross-board affiliations between special committee members and controlling founders will be evaluated under enhanced evidentiary rigor.
