> **Executive Briefing Memorandum**  
> The Federal Trade Commission and DOJ Antitrust Division issued unanimous final rules overhauling the Hart-Scott-Rodino premerger review process. Promulgated under 15 U.S.C. § 18a and 16 C.F.R. Parts 801 and 803, the framework mandates comprehensive ordinary-course pipeline disclosures and labor market cross-assessments, increasing initial merger filing compliance costs across Wall Street transactions by $350,000 per transaction.

---

### Federal Antitrust Rulemaking: FTC Matter No. P239300

In the most consequential structural overhaul of U.S. merger enforcement in over forty-five years, the **Federal Trade Commission (FTC)**, in concurrence with the **Antitrust Division of the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ)**, issued final amendments to the premerger notification rules governing the Hart-Scott-Rodino (HSR) Antitrust Improvements Act of 1976.

The finalized package replaces the streamlined notification format with an extensive evidentiary disclosure regime. Dealmakers navigating reportable transactions exceeding the statutory threshold must now compile extensive internal ordinary-course records, non-competition agreements, and strategic supply chain documentation prior to filing initial notification forms.

### Primary Regulatory Expansions & Filing Burdens

Under the revised regulations codified in Title 16 of the Code of Federal Regulations, acquiring and target entities face substantial compliance obligations:
- **Item 4(c) and 4(d) Expansion**: Parties must disclose internal periodic business plans, synergy presentations, and pipeline product roadmaps prepared within one year of filing that address competition or market shares, regardless of whether created specifically for the transaction.
- **Labor Market Competition Disclosures**: Merging parties must identify overlapping worker categories using Standard Occupational Classification (SOC) codes and disclose geographic employee density data to enable agency assessment of labor monopsony risks.
- **Minority Cross-Holdings & Board Observers**: Transparency mandates require disclosure of 5% or greater minority limited partners, co-investors, and credit facility lenders holding board observer or management consultation rights.

### Transaction Timelines & M&A Deal Architecture

The regulatory modifications significantly reshape transaction economics, merger agreement covenants, and antitrust closing risk:
- **Pre-Filing Lead Time**: Lead times required to assemble HSR initial filings will expand from the historic 10–14 business days to an estimated 45–60 business days.
- **Contractual "Drop Dead" Dates**: Outside closing dates in definitive merger agreements are being extended by 3 to 6 months to accommodate elongated regulatory review periods.
- **Ticking Fees & Reverse Termination Structures**: Arbitrage desks report heightened negotiation of graduated reverse termination fees and monthly "ticking fee" interest payments compensating targets for prolonged regulatory pendency.
