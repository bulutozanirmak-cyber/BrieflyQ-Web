> **Executive Briefing Memorandum (BLUF)**  
> Overturning a lower dismissal, the 2nd U.S. Circuit Court ruled that premature recognition of cloud cluster pre-orders satisfies Section 10(b) scienter standards when top executives executed concurrent $45M in 10b5-1 executive stock dispositions.

---

### Procedural Context: 2nd Circuit Appeals Docket No. 24-1189

In a consequential decision for semiconductor and infrastructure cloud providers, the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit reversed the Southern District of New York's dismissal of a consolidated securities fraud class action brought under Section 10(b) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 and SEC Rule 10b-5.

Writing for a unanimous three-judge panel, Circuit Judge Raymond Lohier held that customary Private Securities Litigation Reform Act (PSLRA) safe-harbor warnings regarding forward-looking revenue projections do not insulate corporate officers who possess internal engineering reports disproving delivery capacity.

### The Accounting Mechanics & Scienter Standard

The core allegations center on ASC 606 revenue recognition standards:
- **Contract Fulfillment**: Defendants booked $380 million in recognized compute revenues upon signing customer reservation letters, prior to physical cluster power-on and interconnect verification.
- **Insider Stock Transactions**: During the exact quarter where revenue growth was touted as accelerating 140% year-over-year, three C-suite executives modified their Rule 10b5-1 pre-arranged trading plans, liquidating an aggregate **$45 million** in common equity.
- **The Scienter Inference**: The Second Circuit ruled that suspicious timing in 10b5-1 modifications—executed within 14 days of an undisclosed supplier stop-ship notice—creates the requisite "cogent and compelling" inference of fraudulent intent under the Supreme Court's *Tellabs* standard.

### Wall Street Defense Exposure

The ruling remands the litigation to District Judge Jed Rakoff for discovery. Defense counsel warned that this decision severely restricts tech issuers' reliance on boilerplate safe-harbor cautionary statements when capital expenditure forecasts deviate from production yield metrics.
