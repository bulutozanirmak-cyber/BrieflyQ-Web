> **Executive Briefing Memorandum**  
> The FTC issued formal 6(b) compulsory orders examining whether exclusive compute allocation agreements evade Hart-Scott-Rodino notification requirements. Regulators warned of daily civil penalties exceeding $50,120 per violation for structural circumvention.

---

### Regulatory Authority: Section 6(b) FTC Act Inquiries

The Federal Trade Commission has expanded its administrative investigation into partnerships formed between tier-one cloud hyperscalers and generative AI foundation model startups. 

Under Section 6(b) of the Federal Trade Commission Act, the agency possesses sweeping power to conduct market studies requiring companies to submit confidential business records, internal board communications, and compute provisioning agreements without filing a formal complaint in federal district court.

### The Hart-Scott-Rodino Evasion Doctrine

The agency’s core regulatory focus is whether non-voting preferred stock structures and commercial partnership covenants function as economic equivalents of voting securities acquisitions:
- **Statutory Threshold**: Transactions crossing the $119.5 million Hart-Scott-Rodino (HSR) reporting threshold require pre-merger notification and mandatory statutory waiting periods.
- **De Facto Operational Control**: Regulators highlighted governance clauses granting hyperscalers "exclusive right of first refusal" on compute clusters and non-voting board observer seats.
- **Civil Penalties**: Under 15 U.S.C. § 18a(g)(1), failure to file required HSR notifications subjects corporations to civil penalties of up to **$50,120 per day** for each day the violation continues, alongside potential structural unwinding demands.

### Impact on Sovereign and Corporate Strategic JVs

The inquiry signals heightened antitrust risks for cross-border AI consortia involving sovereign investment authorities and US technology platforms. M&A counsel are already advising clients to insert expanded regulatory break clauses and explicit HSR compliance indemnities into cloud hosting contracts.
