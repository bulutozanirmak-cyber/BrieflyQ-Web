> **Executive Briefing Memorandum**  
> The Delaware Chancery Court ordered evidentiary discovery into Paramount Global’s $8 billion merger with Skydance Media. Vice Chancellor Zurn ruled that dual-class voting disparity and controller payouts require Entire Fairness review under DGCL Section 141, threatening enforceability of a pre-scheduled $400 million reverse termination break fee across institutional merger arbitrage desks.

---

### Procedural Ruling: Chancery Docket C.A. No. 2024-0419-MTZ

In an unsealed bench order issued in Wilmington, Vice Chancellor Morgan T. Zurn of the Delaware Court of Chancery denied defendants’ motion to dismiss consolidated class action complaints brought by non-voting Class B shareholders of Paramount Global (`NASDAQ: PARA`).

Target plaintiffs allege that the two-step transaction—in which David Ellison’s Skydance Media acquires Shari Redstone’s controlling vehicle National Amusements Inc. (NAI) for $2.4 billion cash before merging into Paramount in an **$8.0 billion** equity consolidation—siphoned premium value away from public equity holders in direct violation of fiduciary loyalty covenants.

### The *MFW* Framework & Controller Conflicts

The legal core of the litigation revolves around Delaware’s rigorous *Kahn v. M&F Worldwide Corp. (MFW)* standard:
- **Procedural Safeguards**: To secure deferential Business Judgment Review instead of stringent Entire Fairness scrutiny, a controlling stockholder transaction must be conditioned *ab initio* on both an independent special committee recommendation and an uncoerced majority-of-the-minority stockholder vote.
- **Vote Disenfranchisement**: Because Class B shares hold zero voting power, plaintiffs argue that the special committee's approval without a standalone Class B advisory vote fails to satisfy *MFW* safe-harbor protections.
- **Board Conflict Discovery**: The court permitted immediate document discovery into communications between special committee financial advisers (Centerview Partners) and Skydance regarding alternative takeover proposals from Apollo Global Management.

### Arbitrage Spreads & Reverse Termination Economics

Institutional merger arbitrage desks are calibrating downside downside scenarios:
- **Gross Spread Stability**: Class B shares trade at a tight $1.15 gross discount (+110 bps) against the $15.00 cash buyout option, signaling high institutional confidence in eventual transaction closing.
- **Break Fee Scrutiny**: The agreement provides a **$400 million** reverse break fee payable by the Skydance buying consortium should closing conditions fail, alongside a $400 million Paramount company break fee.
- **Judicial Exposure**: While monetary settlements or dividend top-ups remain the customary outcome in Delaware dual-class litigation, Vice Chancellor Zurn warned that structural disclosure remedies could necessitate supplemental shareholder proxy circulars prior to closing.
